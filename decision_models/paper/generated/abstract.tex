Financial document triage trades reduced analyst reading against missed information and processing cost. We compare seven decision-model, finance-encoder and lexical systems using binary language-model reference labels. The SEC-dominated corpus contains 25,000 training, 1,000 selection, 500 calibration and 2,000 test documents. Core trained systems share 4,000 documents. Laya LoRA achieves macro F1 0.7843 versus 0.7645 for word TF-IDF/logistic; the paired 95\% difference interval $[-0.0022, 0.0423]$ includes zero. They miss 268 and 188 of 1543 review-worthy references while skipping 32.4\% and 24.6\% of documents. Laya's warm mean inference latency is 64.9 times that of the lexical classifier. Nested training-size comparisons and calibration-selected policies characterize adaptation and the workload--miss tradeoff. Results measure teacher agreement on previously inspected test documents; they do not establish analyst-validated materiality or independent generalization. We release predictions and independent CPU replay.
